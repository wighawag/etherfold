Two tickers of one app never work at once (ADR-0101): every tick runs under a per-app LEASE held in the app's `consumers` row (new columns `lease_owner` and `lease_expires`; `setup` refuses a database whose `consumers` table predates them, with no migration). The first tick of an app creates its row and takes the lease; while another ticker holds it, `tick()` does nothing and reports `{lease: 'not-leased'}`. `TickReport` is now `{lease: 'held', ...RoundReport}`, `{lease: 'not-leased'}` or `{lease: 'lost', reason}` (a round stopped at a fence), and the old fields live on the new `RoundReport`. The lease lasts `leaseMs` (60 s when absent, `DEFAULT_LEASE_MS`), a new option of `createTriggerService` and of each app in the nodejs platform's config, and is renewed under half of it, about one write per 30 s per app; the renewal writes only the lease. Before each call (the gate, a feed read, a predicate, a delivery) the tick renews under half the lease and stops under the fence margin (`callTimeoutMs` plus 5 s for the commit and 5 s for clock skew); a timeout whose margin is not under half the lease is refused at start, naming both. Every statement a tick commits or settles is guarded by the lease, so a stale holder changes nothing (a `LeaseLostError`). `run` releases the lease when its signal aborts, `service.lease.release()` releases it for a one-shot host, and `etherfold-triggers tick` releases it at the end of its round (the platform's `NodeService` gains `release(only?)`). New exports: `createLease`, `LeaseLostError`, `DEFAULT_LEASE_MS`, `LEASE_COMMIT_ALLOWANCE_MS`, `LEASE_CLOCK_SKEW_ALLOWANCE_MS`, and the types `Lease`, `LeaseGuard`, `LeaseColumns` and `RoundReport`; `createConsumer` and `createDeliverer` take an optional `lease`, `registry.matcher` an optional `fence` and `registry.expire` an optional `{lease}`.
