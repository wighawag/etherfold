# A promotion candidate is ARMED by `add`, and drop-on-promotion never drops a stream's WRITER

The promotion policy decides when the **canonical pointer** moves ON ITS OWN: `on-catch-up` (the default in every runtime), `immediate` (opt-in) and `manual` (`a-reconfigure-is-not-an-outage`, stories 1, 3, 13 and 14). Two questions the policy could not avoid have answers that are surprising from the code alone, so they are recorded here: **which generation is a candidate for an automatic promotion**, and **what drop-on-promotion may actually drop**.

**A generation becomes a candidate when it is ADDED beside a live one, and the arming lives in memory.** It is deliberately NOT "every non-canonical generation that is level with the canonical one is a candidate", which is the rule the trigger's arithmetic alone would give. That rule re-promotes a successor on the very next cycle after a REVERT -- a generation the pointer was moved BACK from is caught up by construction -- and story 4's whole claim is that the way back holds. So `Indexer.add` arms, a pointer move disarms, and `open` arms nothing: the generations an indexer is opened with are the set it holds, and which of them is canonical is the registry's durable answer. It is in memory for the same reason a pause is (ADR-0045): the registry records what a generation IS, and being a candidate is what a container is DOING with one.

> **AMENDED 2026-09-19 (`promotion-arms-from-the-slot-so-a-restart-can-finish-an-upgrade`): on the RECEIVING container the arming is a ROW, and `open` arms what the `successor` slot names.** The RULE above is unchanged and is the reason the slot exists: arming is still deliberately not "every non-canonical generation that is level", precisely because that re-promotes what a revert moved away from. What is superseded is WHERE the fact lives and WHEN it may be read. ADR-0084 records durably what a generation is FOR, so `ReceivingIndexer` arms exactly what `successor` names -- read from the registry, by any process, after any restart -- and the in-memory `candidates` set and the `opened` gate are DELETED rather than kept beside it. A generation `predecessor` names is never armed under any policy value, which is this ADR's hazard closed by a row instead of by silence, and it is measured: with that clause removed, a restart after a revert under `immediate` re-promotes what was reverted away from (`packages/cli/test/aRestartFinishesTheUpgrade.test.ts`). The gate on `open` could not be kept, because it made the upgrade story unreachable on the path a developer actually takes -- a generation registered at `open` was never armed, so restarting with a changed processor stalled for ever. The chain-facing `Indexer` is UNCHANGED and still arms in memory at `add`: it holds engines and reads their cursors, so nothing about it is stalled by the paragraph above; unifying it is a separate piece of work.

**Drop-on-promotion never drops a generation that WRITES a stream another held generation follows.** Which generation writes a shared stream is the first one held on it and never the canonical one (ADR-0044), precisely so that a promotion does not hand the append duty to a different engine mid-flight -- so dropping the retired writer would leave the newly canonical generation folding a stream nothing appends to, and the app would simply stop advancing, silently. The drop is therefore DECLINED and logged rather than taken.

## Considered Options

- **Arm every non-canonical generation, statelessly.** Rejected above: it undoes a revert on the next cycle. It is otherwise attractive, because it needs no bookkeeping and survives a reload.
- **Record candidacy DURABLY in the registry**, so a successor created in one session goes on catching up and promotes in the next. Rejected for now: it makes a revert non-durable unless the revert is ALSO recorded, which is a second durable decision and a registry field neither the browser's memory registry nor the server tier has asked for. The cost of not having it is that a successor held across a reload needs an explicit `add` (which RESOLVES the existing generation and re-arms it) or an explicit `promote`.
- **Decide promotion-versus-revert on `createdAt`.** Rejected on the code: `createdAt` is `Date.now()` and two generations registered in the same millisecond compare EQUAL, which the registry breaks on the identity -- a fine total order for a listing an operator reads, and no basis at all for deciding whether to DELETE one. Whether the pointer has ever named a generation is the exact question and is not a clock.
- **Let drop-on-promotion drop the writer and hand the append duty to the promoted generation.** Rejected as out of shape rather than as wrong: it re-opens ADR-0044's rule (a follower is determined by its stream, and the writer is stable by registration order), it means rebuilding an engine that was handed a read-only stream view, and it would have to happen mid-flight. If the storage bound it buys is ever needed for the same-stream case, THAT is the design to weigh, not a special case here.
- **Refuse (throw) instead of declining the drop.** Rejected: the drop is taken inside an advance cycle that a driver calls on a timer, so a throw would surface as an indexing failure for a configuration choice, and the honest outcome (the generation is kept) is not an error.

## Consequences

- **A successor created in a previous session does not auto-promote after a reload.** It goes on catching up if the container was opened with it, and the pointer moves when a caller `add`s it again (which re-arms) or promotes it explicitly. A durable registry plus a durable candidacy record is what would change this.

  > **AMENDED 2026-09-19 (`promotion-arms-from-the-slot-so-a-restart-can-finish-an-upgrade`): on the RECEIVING container it DOES, and the durable candidacy record is the `successor` slot.** This consequence named exactly what was missing, and the option it came from ("record candidacy DURABLY", rejected above because it would make a revert non-durable unless the revert were also recorded) is what ADR-0084 took: `successor` says what is pending and `predecessor` says what a revert returned to, so both halves are durable and neither is inferred. A successor registered at `open` is therefore armed, caught up and promoted with nobody asking. Reaching it needed two more things than the arming, and they are worth naming because neither is visible from this ADR: the TRIGGER had to be evaluable with no held fold for the canonical generation (its position is read through an injected per-generation seam, `GenerationRegistryPort.readStateCursor`, and never through a processor), and something on `run` had to CALL the settle in a shape that holds no follower.
- **Under `immediate`, the retained previous generation is dropped LATER, or not at all.** The deferred drop waits for the successor to reach the cursor the previous generation had at the promotion, and if that successor shares the previous generation's stream, the writer rule declines the drop when it comes due. Both are retention, which is the safe direction.
- **`dropOnPromotion` therefore bounds storage for the FILTER-change case and not for the processor-change case.** A retired generation on a stream of its own goes, and its stream is reaped with it; a retired generation that is still writing the stream its successor folds stays. That is the opposite of what "the common reconfigure is a processor change" would suggest, and it is why this is written down.
- **`Indexer.promote` is never gated by any of this.** The policy governs the move the container makes on its own; `manual` means "only when asked", and moving the pointer back is always available.
