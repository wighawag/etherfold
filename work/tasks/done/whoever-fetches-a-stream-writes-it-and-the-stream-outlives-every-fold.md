## What to build

The substance of ADR-0087. Read the ADR first, INCLUDING its two 2026-09-19 amendments, which exist because this task's first shape was built and measured and the ADR's own text turned out to be wrong in two places.

**This task was two tasks and is now one, because the two halves are mutually entangled.** The narrow fix (a restarted generation FOLLOWS its stream instead of re-fetching the chain) was tasked separately, built, and stopped: it works, and it makes the deployment stop indexing the chain for ever, because a follower has no receiver and so nothing fetches. The reverse split fails too: moving the write duty while a restarted generation is still a fetching receiver puts the fetcher's position back on that generation's empty state, which either re-appends a range the stream already covers or leaves a hole. Under "no generation fetches" the `follows` question DISSOLVES -- every generation folds the stream, the deployment fetches -- so it is one change. `docs/spikes/a-restarted-generation-re-folds-its-stream-instead-of-re-fetching-the-chain/` holds the patch and the numbers; the derivation half in it is small, correct, and worth starting from rather than rewriting.

### Half one: the write duty and the FETCH come off the generation

Today the thing that appends to a stored stream is one generation per stream -- the one `writerOf` ELECTS by registration order -- and that third role is an accident of where the call sits. It is the root of a measured data-loss defect: the elected writer can be a generation this process holds no fold for, so the duty belongs to something absent and nothing appends while a present fold folds happily.

Under ADR-0087 the deployment that FETCHES a stream appends to it and every generation READS it. So `writesStream`, `writerOf`'s role as an ELECTION, and `reconcileWriters` all go. The ADR is explicit that `writerOf` may SURVIVE as the answer to "who fetched this", but nothing derives a DUTY from registration order any more.

**The part that makes this work, and the part the first attempt proved cannot be skipped: the fetch is driven by the DEPLOYMENT, not by a fold's indexing loop.** ADR-0087 names this under Consequences as "the real work of the proposal". A deployment whose every held fold is a follower must still fetch; today it cannot, because fetching is a side effect of a receiver advancing. Whatever answers `expectedFromBlock` must be the STREAM's position rather than any one fold's, or a restarted deployment with empty state asks for history the stream already holds. The ADR says where that number lives: `streamLastToBlock` comes from the keeper's own stored cursor, and whoever fetches can read it from the keeper directly.

Three things make this smaller than it sounds, and the ADR checked all three against the code: the appender is ALREADY built from the database handle and the indexer NAME with no generation in it, so what exists is a GATE deciding which fold holds the pen; the two numbers that keep a stream honest both belong to whoever fetches; and it STRENGTHENS ADR-0044, because under it no generation fetches, so every generation's state is a function of the stream by construction.

### Half two: a stream outlives every fold over it

A stream is reaped today "where no registered generation is left folding it", and that reaping rides the operator's `reclaim` verb AND an automatic path: registering into an occupied `successor` slot drops the replaced generation, and its stream with it if nothing else folds it. Save twice in a tab and the second save can delete a stream.

Remove the AUTOMATIC reap. `reclaim` and `deleteStream` keep working exactly as they do, guards and all. What goes is deletion nobody asked for.

**This half is a requirement, not a nicety, and it is the thing here most likely to be quietly under-built.** The stream is what chain fetches BOUGHT. State is derived and recomputable from it; a stream can only be re-fetched from a node that frequently refuses historical ranges. "No registered generation folds it" is precisely the state a stream is in BETWEEN an old fold being dropped and a new one being built, which is when its value is highest.

**A third automatic deleter exists and ADR-0087 does not name it. Do not let it undo this.** `openGenerationRegistry` runs a SWEEP on every open that drops any stream subtree "claimed by no registered generation". A stream kept after its last generation is dropped is, by that rule, exactly an orphan -- so removing the reap alone would keep the stream for the life of the process and lose it at the next restart. That passes a green gate and is wrong, in the precise window the keep exists for. Read the sweep's own reasoning first: its stated purpose is collecting subtrees written before generations existed, which no departure could fire a reap for, and that purpose is untouched by this. Work out what makes a deliberately-kept stream DISTINGUISHABLE from a pre-generation orphan across a restart, and build that.

### The corrected latent-hazard fix

ADR-0087 asked for `streamCanReceive()` to refuse on an unknown stream position. **Measured, that is two facts and only one may refuse** -- see the ADR's amendment. `!this.lastSync` refusing is inert and may land. `streamLastToBlock === undefined` must STAY PERMISSIVE: it is the documented absence of a stream, not an unknown position, and refusing there declines the first save of every fresh deployment and reds two `streamSeedInstall.test.ts` cases by name. The duplicate the ADR attributed to this method was never on its path: it was on `StreamBuilder.storeStream` over an append-only `EmissionAppender`, which has no hole guard at all. **That is where the guard belongs, and this task is the one that moves the write duty, so it owns putting it there.**

**Do NOT introduce an elected-writer POINTER**, meaning a mutable record naming the current writer. ADR-0087 rejects it explicitly: it turns a derivable fact into a coordinated one, two processes holding different folds can disagree, it can oscillate, `writerOf` stops being a pure function every reader evaluates independently, and delete-succession stops being atomic with the delete.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Nothing derives a WRITE DUTY from registration order: `writesStream` as a per-fold gate and `reconcileWriters` are gone, and no replacement elects a writer among generations.
- [ ] A deployment whose every held fold is a FOLLOWER still fetches and still appends. Asserted on the chain reads: the failure this replaces was a restarted deployment asking the node for `["eth_chainId"]` and nothing else, for ever.
- [ ] A deployment restarted over the same database with a changed processor RE-FOLDS the stored stream rather than re-fetching it: zero historical `eth_getLogs` for a range the stream already covers, asserted on the wire and not on a flag.
- [ ] No range is stored twice: the deployment's fetch position comes from the STREAM, not from any one fold's state, so an empty-state successor cannot drag it backwards.
- [ ] A generation that folds without fetching appends nothing, asserted on a deployment holding TWO folds at once.
- [ ] Registering into an occupied `successor` slot no longer deletes a stream, and the generation's own state namespace is still dropped as it is today.
- [ ] A stream kept because nothing folds it SURVIVES A RESTART, asserted by re-opening over the same database and finding the bytes. A test that only re-reads within one process does not cover this.
- [ ] A pre-generation orphan subtree is still collected, so the sweep's own reason for existing is not traded away.
- [ ] `reclaim` still deletes streams when an operator asks, with its per-generation decline intact, and `deleteStream` keeps its guard. Both asserted.
- [ ] The append-only writer gains the hole guard `streamCanReceive` was wrongly credited with, and `streamLastToBlock === undefined` stays permissive where it means "no stream on disk".
- [ ] `build` no longer fails outright on a follower-only restart: the three `aRerunBuildSettlesItsPointer` cases stay green.
- [ ] The eleven existing cases that encode today's behaviour are each re-scoped deliberately -- five in `receivingContainer.test.ts`, six across `aChangedContextCreatesASuccessor`, `aRerunBuildSettlesItsPointer`, `aSuccessorLandsInADurableSlot` and `anIndexProcessAdvancesItsSuccessor` -- and which were changed and why is stated. Do not soften the change to keep them green, and do not delete one silently.
- [ ] Operator-facing text explaining that a fold is not writing its stream because an older generation is, is removed or corrected.
- [ ] `CONTEXT.md` is updated where it states the retired rule, for the concepts THIS task changes. The family's closing task owns the final coherence pass.
- [ ] Tests cover the new behaviour, mirroring the repo's existing test style.
- [ ] A changeset accompanies the change (`pnpm changeset`).

## Prompt

The goal is that a stored stream has exactly one writer, that the writer is the DEPLOYMENT fetching it rather than one of the folds reading it, and that a stream is never deleted merely because the last fold over it went away.

Read ADR-0087 in full including both amendments. Then ADR-0044 for the one-writer rule this supersedes in part, ADR-0038 for why the ENGINE is the arbiter of whether an append is safe and which two numbers that needs, and ADR-0084 for slots, which this does not touch. `CONTEXT.md` carries the retired rule in several glossary entries. Start from `docs/spikes/a-restarted-generation-re-folds-its-stream-instead-of-re-fetching-the-chain/`: its README is the measurement and its patch is a working derivation half, including a `replaceTheSuccessor` split into a pure plan and a performing half so the derivation can read the records as they will STAND while the refusal still leaves the registry untouched. That ordering constraint is real and was measured; do not lose it.

**The obvious fix is wrong and that was MEASURED rather than argued.** Handing the write duty to the fold that happens to be present stores the history a second time: on the defect's own scenario `_emissions` ended with 4 rows where 2 were correct. Nor can it be fixed by timing the hand-over: a reconciliation once per fetch cycle observes the fold BELOW the coverage and then ABOVE it and never ON it, and handing over below duplicates while handing over above leaves a HOLE. There is no observation point that is neither. If you find yourself building a hand-over, you have taken the rejected option.

**The second measured trap is the one that stopped this task's first shape.** Making the restarted generation a follower without moving the FETCH gives a deployment that re-folds, is promoted, serves reads, reports healthy, and never fetches a block again. Before you finish, run the deployment's restart scenario and look at the METHODS it asked the node for. If the answer is `["eth_chainId"]`, you have rebuilt the failure.

The third: work out where a stream is deleted TODAY, enumerate every path, and decide each explicitly. The sweep on registry open is the one the ADR does not mention and the one that silently undoes this across a restart.

The fourth: `writerOf` is not simply deleted. It may remain as the answer to "who fetched this stream". Decide what survives and make sure no caller reads it as a duty.

Done means: no generation holds a write duty, a follower-only deployment still fetches and appends, a restart re-folds without re-fetching and without storing anything twice, a stream survives having no folds over it including across a restart, and the operator's delete verbs are untouched.

## Decisions

**What drives the fetch, and where its position comes from.** The `ReceivingIndexer` holds one `StreamWriter` per stream it holds a fold on, built with the first fold on that stream and never rebuilt. Its position is `getFromBlock` over the stream's stored coverage claim, and its unconfirmed window is walked out of the stream's own tail rather than borrowed from a fold — so ADR-0038's arbiter moved *with* the duty and an empty-state successor cannot drag the fetch backwards. Alternative considered and rejected: keeping the fetch on a fold and only moving the appender, which is the hand-over ADR-0087 measured as duplicating or holing. Touches: `ReceivingIndexerOptions` now needs `streamCursor` beside `appendEmissions` and `replay`, and a container missing any of the three is refused at `open` (`refuseContainerThatCannotFetch` / `refuseFoldWithNoStream`) rather than folding for ever on stale history — which is the failure ADR-0087's second amendment measured.

**A fold advances two ways through one implementation.** After the append the writer offers the delta to every fold on that stream; a fold that is level applies it there and then, one that is behind declines and is carried by its bounded rebuild. I did this rather than making `rebuildMore` the only path because *every* host would otherwise have to schedule a rebuild to fold anything at all, and the server package schedules none — an HTTP-only deployment would have ingested and never folded. Per-fold advances are serialised through one promise chain (`ReceivingIndexer.advance`), because a live delta and a scheduled chunk both decide from the same durable checkpoint and an overlap would meet the storage seam's duplicate-height refusal.

**`writerOf` survives, renamed.** It is now `fetcherOf` on the module and on `GenerationRegistry`, with the derivation unchanged (oldest surviving generation on a stream, registration order, stored nowhere). The rename is what makes "no caller reads it as a duty" structural rather than a comment. Alternative: keep the name with corrected docs — rejected, because the chain-facing container still derives `follows` from it and a reader would have had to hold two meanings of one word.

**The chain-facing `Indexer` (browser) is deliberately NOT restructured, and it still derives `follows` from `fetcherOf`.** There the thing that fetches a stream *is* a generation (`IndexerGeneration` opens `load()` with `eth_chainId`; a batch is a side effect of it advancing), so "which generation fetched this" and "which generation writes it" are one fact, which is ADR-0044's follower rule and is untouched. ADR-0087's Consequences name `run` and `build` as the restructure and say nothing about the browser engine. **A reviewer should know the cost:** a browser tab reloaded with a changed processor is in the same shape the receiving side was — the elected fetcher is a generation the tab holds no fold for — so the same class of stall is reachable there. Splitting `IndexerGeneration`'s fetch from its fold is a separate change nothing has asked for, and I did not smuggle it in.

**How a kept stream is told from a pre-generation orphan across a restart.** A durable record in the registry's own records (`GenerationRegistryState.keptStreams`; `_generation_streams` on SQL, a `kept` key level on IndexedDB, a set in the memory port), written when a generation is registered on a stream and removed only by an asked-for deletion. The sweep compares a stored subtree against the generations *and* that set. Alternatives considered: inferring it from the presence of a `_stream_coverage` row (substrate-specific, implicit, and it would make the sweep collect nothing on SQL), and dropping the sweep (trades away its own stated purpose). Deliberately **not** counted by `maxStreams`, which goes on counting distinct streams among registered generations, so keeping a stream brings no deployment closer to a refusal. Touches: the schema (a new fixed table in `db.sql`, no version bump — nothing is published) and every `GenerationRegistryPort` implementation.

**Every deletion path, enumerated and decided.**
1. `replaceTheSuccessor` → `dropReplaced` (registering into an occupied `successor` slot): row + state namespace, **no reap**. This is the path the ADR names.
2. `dropSuperseded` (drop-on-promotion): row + state namespace, **no reap**. Also automatic, also nobody asked.
3. `reclaim` (operator verb): row + state namespace + **reap** when no generation is left folding the stream. The one caller that asks.
4. `registry.deleteStream` (operator verb): unchanged, guard intact — **widened** to accept a stream the registry records with no generation on it, because otherwise exactly the streams the keep exists for would be undeletable. `UnknownStreamError` now means "this indexer has no such stream at all".
5. The **sweep on registry open**: keeps its purpose, now compares against the stream records too.
6. Generation caps: refuse, never evict — untouched.
7. The chain-facing `Indexer`'s own drops (`dropSuperseded`, `dropReplaced`): they call the same `deleteGeneration`, so they stopped reaping too, which is consistent with the ADR and costs a browser tab disk it can reclaim by asking.

**A behaviour change on an opt-in flag, worth a reviewer's eye.** With no duty to strand and no reap, `dropOnPromotion` now really does drop the superseded generation on an ordinary processor upgrade, where the strand clause used to decline it. The flag does what it says; what the deployment keeps instead is the stream, which is the expensive half. `DeclinedReclaim` loses its `writes-a-followed-stream` reason (the per-generation `deletion-failed` decline is intact).

**A latent replay bug the change made systematic, fixed at the source.** `generateStreamFromReplay` de-duplicates a re-offered block by window membership, which cannot answer "did this fold already hold it and take it back" — so a catch-up reaching back over its own reorg window re-applied the dead branch at a height its replacement occupies (`UNIQUE constraint failed: _blocks.number`). A block applied *and* retracted within one walk, at or below where the fold has already folded through, is now delivered neither way; the `lastToBlock` half of that condition is what keeps a rebuild from a fresh cursor reproducing the live run exactly (ADR-0042, asserted by `replay.test.ts`). A second short-circuit in `GenerationRebuild.more` skips a chunk when the stream has not moved.

**One narrow tolerance in `GenerationRebuild.follow`.** A live delta whose events carry no raw half is folded as delivered instead of being refused, because `reparse` needs `topics`/`data`/`address` and a capture predating the stream-seam narrowing has none (`BASE_ABANDONED` in `@etherfold/conformance-workload-stratagems`). The delta a deployment just derived is already decoded under the source it is running now. The asymmetry is deliberate and loud: the same rows read back off *disk* have no decoded half to fall back on, so `more()` still reports `undecodable`.

**The eleven tests, each re-scoped.** Five in `receivingContainer.test.ts`: the container now gets a stored stream and the fixture gets real topics (a decoded-only log is one no fold can replay); the `writesStream`/`follows` assertions became "neither fold has either, and the live context is the stream's writer"; the "second fold with no `replay`" refusal became a refusal at `open` (the asymmetry went with the election) plus a new case that a container which could fetch nothing is refused; and **`expectedFromBlock` on a successor over a written database changed from `START_BLOCK` to the stream's resume point — that number is the change**. Six elsewhere: `aChangedContextCreatesASuccessor` (3 cases — the successor no longer asks for a range the stream holds, and it catches up by re-folding; its fixture gained real topics and a `replay` source), `aRerunBuildSettlesItsPointer` (1 — a build stopped from outside no longer leaves a level successor, because the exit work is what carries it; the claim it exists for, that the pointer did not move, is unchanged; the other two cases stayed green), `aSuccessorLandsInADurableSlot` (2 — the replaced successor's stream is kept, and the strand decline became "it is dropped and the stream survives"), `anIndexProcessAdvancesItsSuccessor` (1 — the case that relied on the wire to advance a restarted successor now asserts the stronger thing: with no tick the successor does not advance *at all*, and the tick is what does both). Beyond the eleven: `writerSuccession.test.ts` is re-scoped whole and kept at its path, because it asserts the same *situation* with the opposite answer — deleting a generation hands over nothing, because nothing was held; `aGenerationNoSlotNamesIsReclaimed`, `theRebuildReplaysTheLocalStream`, `aRebuildInProgressIsVisible`, `equivalence`, `entityStore`, `theCanonicalPointerMovesBack`, `theReceivingContainerPublishesWhatItApplied` and `rebuild` were adjusted where `rebuildMore` now reports one entry per fold rather than per follower. New: `packages/cli/test/aRestartReFoldsTheStoredStream.test.ts` (the methods asked, the ranges asked for, the row count, and two folds at once) and `packages/core/test/streamWriter.test.ts` (the position and the hole guard), plus keep/sweep cases on both durable registries.

**`ADR-0087` is marked implemented and carries a fourth amendment** recording the four things it did not say: the sweep, `deleteStream`'s widening, the declines that dissolve with `follows`, and the replay bug. No new ADR: every decision above is this ADR's substance or a correction to it, and a second number would fork the record.
